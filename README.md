A complete story about a journey, told in exactly one sentence: a ferryman who spent thirty years rowing strangers across the river and never crossing himself, until his daughter rows him over to his father's grave. Built for [make.fun](https://make.fun)'s "One Sentence, One Journey" bounty — the entry's single twist is that the carrier becomes the carried, and the arrival is grief, not geography.

> For thirty years the ferryman rowed strangers across the river at dawn and never once set foot on the far bank himself, until the morning his daughter took the oars from his hands, rowed him across, and set him down in the wet grass beside his father's grave, which had waited for him all along.

## Why it works

Three beats, one sentence: a life spent carrying others across (the journey refused), the reversal when his daughter takes the oars (the carrier becomes the carried), and an arrival that lands on a concrete image — wet grass, a grave that waited — instead of an abstract realisation. No return home, no train, no "finally understood": the destination turns out to be the thing he spent thirty years avoiding.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```

`main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only) writes the story to `/output/entry.txt` — or to `$OUTPUT_PATH` when that is set — and refuses to write anything outside the 1–300 character window the rules allow, or anything that is not exactly one sentence. The output is deterministic: every run writes the same bytes.
